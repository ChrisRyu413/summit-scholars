THE STARTING POINT

Before we plan,
we learn the student.

We begin with the student’s transcript, school curriculum, testing history, activities, interests, time commitments, and family priorities. This diagnostic tells us what is already strong, what is missing, and which goals are ambitious but still realistic.

What families receiveA clear starting-point assessment and a prioritized plan—not a generic checklist.
  • Transcript & curriculum review
  • Strengths, gaps & constraints
  • Goals & family priorities
ACADEMIC DIRECTION

Build an academic plan
that keeps doors open.

Course selection, GPA, AP rigor, and SAT or ACT preparation must support the same university goals. We map these decisions across multiple semesters, balancing ambition with the student’s actual workload and protecting the academic foundation of the application.

What we manageCourse rigor, GPA targets, testing milestones, and the order in which each goal should be tackled.
  • GPA & course rigor
  • AP course planning
  • SAT / ACT strategy
  • Multi-year timeline
OPPORTUNITY DEVELOPMENT

Turn an interest into
firsthand experience.

Students often know what they like but not where to explore it. We research suitable professors, laboratories, clinics, nonprofits, competitions, and summer programs, then help the student prepare personalized outreach, a résumé, follow-ups, and meetings.

Why this mattersThe goal is not to collect activities. It is to test an interest in the real world and discover which direction deserves deeper commitment.
  • Research & internships
  • Shadowing & volunteering
  • Cold outreach
  • Programs & competitions
FROM PARTICIPATION TO OWNERSHIP

Move from joining
to creating.

Participating in an existing program demonstrates interest. Creating something demonstrates initiative. We help the student use what they observed through classes and activities to identify a specific problem worth solving.

The transitionInterest becomes a focused question; the question becomes a proposal; the proposal becomes a project the student genuinely owns.
  • Problem definition
  • Project concept
  • Pitch deck & action plan
  • Prototype direction
PASSION PROJECT EXECUTION

Make the idea work
outside the student’s head.

A Passion Project becomes meaningful only when someone can use it, respond to it, or benefit from it. We guide development of the prototype, identify a pilot audience or partner organization, organize feedback, and help the student improve the work through multiple iterations.

Hands-on supportPrototype development, pilot planning, partnerships, research connections, data collection, presentations, competitions, and iteration.
  • Prototype
  • Pilot & partnerships
  • Feedback & data
  • Improve & present
MEASURABLE IMPACT

Show what changed
because of the work.

Impact does not need to mean thousands of users. It must be specific and credible. We help the student define meaningful outcomes, document participation and feedback, analyze what improved, and decide whether the project should expand to new people or institutions.

What remainsA functioning result, evidence of learning and leadership, measurable outcomes, and a final product or impact report.
  • Outcome measurement
  • Evidence & documentation
  • Scaling strategy
  • Final impact report
APPLICATION STRATEGY

Choose where—and how—
the student should apply.

We build a balanced university list by examining academic fit, intended major, program structure, selectivity, and the student’s preferences. Then we decide how Early Decision, Early Action, Restrictive Early Action, and Regular Decision should be used within one realistic plan.

The strategy includesUniversity and major selection, reach–target–likely balance, application positioning, round selection, and a complete deadline calendar.
  • College & program list
  • Major strategy
  • ED · EA · REA · RD
  • Deadline planning
STORY, APPLICATION & SUBMISSION

Translate years of work
into one credible story.

The application should explain the meaning behind the record without exaggerating it. We guide the personal statement and supplemental essays from brainstorming through revision, refine activity and honors descriptions, prepare interviews, and review every component before submission.

What we reviewPersonal statement, supplemental essays, activities, honors, résumé, additional information, interviews, deadlines, and final submission checks.
  • Essays & supplements
  • Activities & honors
  • Interview preparation
  • Final application review

as the student grows.

We revisit the strategy throughout the relationship instead of forcing the student to follow an outdated plan. Our mentor team collaborates across academics, research, projects, testing, and admissions whenever the student’s next decision requires it.

HOW WE MAKE IT HAPPEN

We Don’t Just Recommend Opportunities. We Help Create Them.

Many valuable opportunities never appear in a public listing. We identify organizations, professors, clinics, laboratories, nonprofits, companies, and professionals aligned with each student’s interests—then help the student approach them strategically.

01

Cold Outreach

Many of the best opportunities are never publicly advertised.

We research relevant contacts and help students develop thoughtful, personalized outreach under their own name.

  • Opportunity research
  • Contact identification
  • Personalized cold emails
  • Resume and introduction materials
  • Follow-up strategy
  • Meeting preparation and next steps

We do not take ownership away from the student. We design and manage the process so the student learns to create opportunities directly.

02

Connections When They Matter

Cold emailing is not the only path.

When appropriate, students may also benefit from mentors’ personal and professional networks—including university and graduate students, researchers, healthcare professionals, organizations, and other mentors.

University Networks Research Healthcare Organizations

A connection can provide a stronger starting point, not a guaranteed placement. Participation always depends on student fit, initiative, availability, and organization approval.

03

A Mentorship Network That Keeps Growing

Our mentors are actively progressing through the academic and professional pathways our students hope to enter.

As our mentors advance through university, dental school, research, and professional education, they gain new firsthand experience and build relationships across academic and professional communities. That current knowledge and expanding network return directly to the students we mentor.

Current, Firsthand Experience

Our guidance is informed by mentors who continue to experience U.S. universities, combined dental programs, pre-dental education, dental school, research, and admissions firsthand.

As Summit Scholars grows, the range of experience, insight, and opportunity available to students grows with it.

LEAVE EVIDENCE OFImpact

A result people can see, use, measure, and remember.

The strongest Passion Projects do not begin with a résumé line. They begin with a question the student genuinely cares about.

We turn that question into a prototype—a tool, program, product, resource, study, or initiative that can be tested.

A real pilot replaces assumptions with feedback from an organization, community, or audience.

Evidence creates direction. The student measures what worked, improves what did not, and documents the result.

The project grows beyond an idea into initiative, leadership, and measurable impact—the center of a credible admissions story.
